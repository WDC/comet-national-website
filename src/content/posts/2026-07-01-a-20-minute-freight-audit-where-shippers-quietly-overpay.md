You do not need a consultant and a month of meetings to find money in your freight budget. Most of the overpaying hides in the same handful of places, and you can spot it in about 20 minutes with a stack of recent invoices, your rate quotes, and a coffee. Here is how to run that check yourself.

Grab your last 20 to 30 freight bills. Put them next to the original quotes. Then work through the sections below. You are looking for the gap between what you were quoted and what you actually paid, and for loads that were forced into the wrong mode.

## 1. Compare the quote to the invoice, line by line

Start with the simplest test: did the bill match the quote? Circle every charge that was not on the original quote. Those surprise lines are where the leak usually starts. A clean quote that turns into a messy invoice is not a pricing problem, it is a visibility problem, and it is costing you.

If you see the same accessorial show up again and again, that is not bad luck. That is a pattern in how your freight is being classed, measured, or handled, and patterns can be fixed.

## 2. Hunt for reclasses and reweighs

Reclasses and reweighs are two of the most common quiet charges in LTL. You quote a class, the carrier inspects the freight, and the bill comes back at a higher class or a heavier weight than you expected. Sometimes the carrier is right and your paperwork was off. Sometimes it is worth disputing.

Either way, if you are getting reclassed repeatedly, the answer is upstream. Tighten your dimensions and weights before the freight moves, and quote what is actually on the dock, not what you hope is on the dock.

## 3. Check for density and linear-foot charges

Across the LTL industry, carriers apply density minimums and linear-foot (cubic-capacity) charges to freight that is long, light, or hard to stack. If your product is low-density or takes up more than its share of a trailer, those charges can raise a shipment's cost substantially.

Look for them by name on your invoices. If long or bulky loads keep triggering linear-foot charges, LTL may simply be the wrong tool. That freight often moves better, and more predictably, on a partial flatbed.

## 4. Find the loads that fought the mode they were on

This is where the biggest money hides. Go through your bills and flag anything that was:

- Long, oversized, or odd-shaped and crammed into standard LTL
- A single pallet or two paying full-truckload money
- A container off the port that you trucked in an inefficient way because nobody staged it
- Temperature-controlled freight riding on a service that did not really fit

Each of those is a mode mismatch. The cheapest quote on the wrong mode is not the cheapest shipment. A 14-foot piece that no big LTL network wants to touch, and keeps surcharging, is a textbook case for open-deck instead.

## 5. Add up your handoff and staging costs

Look at freight that changed hands: rail or ocean to truck, a load that had to be recovered, broken down, and redelivered. Count the detention, the extra moves, and the storage. If a container is sitting because there is no plan to get it off the port and onto trucks, that delay is a cost even when it does not show as a line item.

## What to do with what you found

Add up the surprise charges, the mode mismatches, and the handoff waste. That number is your annual leak, roughly. Now you have a real target instead of a vague sense that freight costs too much.

A few clear positions from people who book loads for a living:

- **The right mode beats the biggest truck.** Match the freight to the service and most accessorials disappear.
- **Predictable pricing is worth real money.** A quote you can trust protects your budget better than a low number that balloons on the invoice.
- **One point of contact removes a whole category of waste.** When one team can move your flatbed, truckload, dry, refrigerated, and partial freight, and stage the handoffs, you stop paying for the seams between vendors.
