The cheapest quote and the cheapest shipment are not the same thing. On low-density, long, or hard-to-handle freight, they are often opposites. The number that wins the quote is a starting bid, and the accessorial charges that follow are where the real price gets set.

None of this is a scam. Every charge below has a reason behind it. The problem is that they show up after you have committed, on an invoice you did not build, for a shipment you thought you had priced. Here is what to watch for.

## Density and class

LTL pricing runs on freight class, and freight class runs on density: weight against the space the shipment occupies. A pallet that is light for its size is low-density, and low-density freight climbs the class scale fast.

If your quote assumed one class and the freight rates out at another, the carrier reclasses it, and the price moves with it. A reclass on a single shipment can change the bill meaningfully, and you find out after delivery. The fix is to measure and weigh honestly before you quote, not to hope the estimate holds.

## Linear-foot and cubic-capacity charges

Long freight has its own trap. When a shipment takes up more than a set stretch of trailer, many carriers apply a linear-foot rule, sometimes called a cubic-capacity charge. The idea is straightforward: your load is blocking space the carrier would rather fill with other freight, so it prices the shipment as if it were much heavier than it is.

For a shipper moving pipe, lumber, or anything that runs long, this is the charge that quietly doubles a number. It is also the clearest signal that the freight might belong on an open deck instead of inside an LTL van.

## Reweighs, redeliveries, and the rest

The smaller charges stack up in the background:

- Reweighs, when the carrier's scale disagrees with your BOL.
- Liftgate and limited-access fees, when there is no dock on one end.
- Redelivery, when the first attempt cannot be received.
- Detention, when a truck waits longer than the free window allows.

Each one is defensible on its own. Together, on the wrong shipment, they are the difference between the quote you accepted and the invoice you dispute.

## Why predictable beats cheap

For a small shipper, a surprise accessorial is not just an annoyance, it is a number that was never in the budget. That is why predictable pricing is worth real money, even when the headline rate is not the lowest one on the screen.

This is the case for partial flatbed on the freight that keeps triggering these charges. When long or low-density freight rides open deck and bills by the space it actually uses, the linear-foot and density games mostly disappear, because the pricing is not fighting the shape of your load in the first place.

## How to protect yourself

You do not need to become a freight-class expert to stop overpaying. Three habits cover most of it:

1. Measure and weigh every shipment before you quote, and write it on the BOL.
2. Ask what triggers a reclass or a linear-foot charge on your specific freight, before it ships.
3. When the same freight keeps drawing accessorials, ask whether it belongs on a different mode entirely.

The cheapest quote is easy to find. The cheapest shipment takes one more question. If you want a second set of eyes on where your freight spend is leaking, that is exactly the kind of look we like to do.
